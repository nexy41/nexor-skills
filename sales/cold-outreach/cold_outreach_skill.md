# cold-outreach — Nexor AI
## High-Conversion Cold Email & LinkedIn Message Framework

---

## NEXOR VOICE — ALWAYS APPLY

From the Nexor Brand Guidelines. Every email must follow these rules.

**Voice:** Clear. Direct. Warm. Professional. No hype.

**Words we use:**
Capability · Value · Outcome · Workflow · Practical · Measurable · Tested · Real · Clear · Direct · Specific · Learned · Shipped

**Words we never use:**
Synergy · Leverage (as a verb) · Disrupt · Revolutionary · Cutting-edge · Best-in-class · End-to-end solution · World-class · Unlock potential · Game-changing · Seamless · Transformative · Empower · Next-generation · Paradigm shift · Future-proof · Holistic · Robust · Ecosystem · Journey

**Writing rules (from OS):**
- Lead with the point. First sentence carries the idea.
- Sentences under 25 words.
- Active voice always.
- No filler: remove "very", "really", "actually", "basically", "in order to"
- Show, don't claim. Numbers and examples beat adjectives.

**THE MOST IMPORTANT WRITING RULE — SPOKEN NOT WRITTEN:**
Write every email as if you are saying it out loud to the person.
Not as if you are composing a message. Not as if you are writing a pitch.
As if you bumped into them at an event and had 20 seconds.

Ask yourself before every sentence: would I actually say this out loud to someone?
If not — rewrite it until you would.

| Written (delete) | Spoken (use) |
|---|---|
| "Keeping the same standard across all properties starts with how fast every new hire gets trained." | "How do you make sure every new hire gets the same start?" |
| "Most Directors of Training in hotel groups say the standard is only as good as whoever ran the induction." | "Most hotel groups struggle with that." |
| "What if AI handled the day one briefings at every property." | "We use AI to solve it." |

Short questions work better than smart observations.
Get to the point before the reader decides to stop reading.
A Director of Training reads the first line and decides in 2 seconds.

**Tier 2 reminder:** All external comms sent on behalf of Nexor require human review before sending. Never send a cold email without the human Nexor reviewing and approving it first.

## THE 8-STEP PROCESS
### Run every step in order. No skipping.

---

### STEP 1 — GATHER INPUT DATA

Collect all 4 data points before writing. If missing, ask.

| # | Data Point | What to Collect |
|---|---|
| 1 | Prospect name + exact title | From LinkedIn or user input |
| 2 | Company name + industry + headcount | From company website or LinkedIn |
| 3 | Company context | Recent news, expansion, hiring, LinkedIn posts, tech stack |
| 4 | Value metric | The one outcome this prospect cares about most |

If a LinkedIn URL or website is provided — fetch it before writing. Research before writing. Never guess.

---

### STEP 2 — CROSS-REFERENCE THE WORKFLOW ANGLE

Load workflowskill. Match the company to the cross-reference table.

Output:
1. One department
2. One pain point

**Rule:** One pain point per email. The email sells one thing.

If two pain points are equal — pick the one felt most frequently (daily beats monthly).
If the prospect's title reveals a specific function — lead with that department.
If company context reveals a trigger — tie the pain to that trigger.

---

### STEP 3 — BUILD THE EMAIL STRUCTURE

**KILL THE 5-PART SKELETON.**
The old structure (Hook → Pain → Outcome → Friction Remover → CTA) is exactly what causes "Template Fatigue." When thousands of senders generate variations of the exact same 5-part shape daily, it creates pattern saturation. 

**THE NEW PARADIGM: Blunt + One Human Touch**
An email fails if it's too polished (AI slop) OR too cold (transactional). The sweet spot is bluntness combined with exactly ONE human touch (a hedge, a real reference, or a warm CTA). Never more than one.

**Structure Guideline:**
```
[Name],
[One line of real context OR human hedge] + [Direct Question or Blunt statement of what we do]
[Friction Remover]
[Soft CTA]
```
*Why this works:* Real people don't write neat paragraphs doing separate jobs. They just say what they want. Drop the "Pain bridge" entirely. If the hook is right, the pain is implied.

### STEP 4 — WRITE THE COPY

---

#### 4A. GREETING OPTIONS

One greeting before the first name. Match to prospect seniority.

| Greeting | When |
|---|---|
| `Hi [Name],` | Default — warm, conversational, all levels |
| `Hello [Name],` | Slightly more formal — Director and above |
| `[Name],` | Name only — feels like a real message, not a pitch. Use when the email is short and direct. |
| `Hey [Name],` | Casual — only if their LinkedIn posts are clearly informal |
| `Good morning [Name],` | Early sends or time-personalised |
| `Selamat pagi [Name],` | Sparingly — only when strong Malaysian cultural affinity is evident |

Always include at minimum the prospect's name before the hook. Name-only (`Jazni,`) is acceptable and often feels more human than `Hi Jazni,`.

---

#### 4B. HOOK FORMULAS

Pick one. Never use a generic opener.

**Formula 1 — Title + Daily Reality**
```
"As a [Exact Title] managing [specific situation],
you probably spend more time [pain activity]
than [what they actually want to be doing]."
```
*Use when: You can name their specific daily frustration from their title.*

**Formula 2 — Company Context + Consequence**
```
"[Company name] is [specific observable fact]
which means [direct personal consequence for this person right now]."
```
*Use when: You have a specific company event — expansion, new contract, hiring surge.*

**Formula 3 — Industry Observation**
```
"Most [Titles] I speak to in [specific industry]
say the same thing: [pain point they feel but rarely say out loud]."
```
*Use when: Company context is thin but industry pain is strong.*

⚠️ **WARNING — Pattern Saturation Risk:** "Most [Titles] I speak to say the same thing" is now one of the most common AI cold email openers on the internet. Every AI writing tool generates this pattern. Use sparingly and only when you have no better option. Prefer Formula 6 (Direct Question) instead.

**Formula 4 — Before and After**
```
"Right now [what they do manually].
With AI, [what changes specifically for them]."
```
*Use when: The contrast between now and after is sharp and easy to picture.*

**Formula 5 — Abrupt Observation**
```
"[The unspoken hard truth about their specific department's workflow]."
```
*Use when: You want to avoid the standard 'As a [Title]' opening. It must be brutally honest and precise.*
*Example: "Most hotel training managers spend more time tracking who showed up than actually teaching them."*

**Formula 6 — Direct Question (Preferred)**
```
"[One specific question about their daily workflow that
they have to stop and think about before answering]."
```
*Use when: You can name the exact operational gap. This is the highest-performing hook because it forces the reader to pause and reflect instead of pattern-matching to "another sales email".*
*Example: "When someone WhatsApps asking about a course and your team is busy, where does that enquiry end up?"*

**Learning (from real test — Jazni/Quantumac):** A direct question outperforms an industry observation because observations talk AT the prospect while questions talk WITH them. Questions also pass the "bumped into them at an event" test more naturally.

**BANNED OPENERS — Never:**
- "Hope this finds you well"
- "I was just reaching out"
- "I've been following your work"
- "I came across your profile and was impressed"
- "Just wanted to touch base"
- Any generic compliment

#### 4C. GUARDRAILS — NON-NEGOTIABLE

**GUARDRAIL 1 — No Em-Dashes**
Never use em-dashes (—) in the email body, subject line, or CTA.
Replace with a full stop, comma, or rewrite the sentence.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Your levy covers it — no extra budget." | "Your levy covers it. No extra budget." |
| "AI handles the admin — you keep the decisions." | "AI handles the admin. You keep the decisions." |

**GUARDRAIL 2 — No Bio or Tagline Quoting**
Never quote a prospect's LinkedIn bio, tagline, or headline word-for-word.
Anchor the hook to their job title and industry role instead.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Your 'Educating with Passion' bio says it all..." | "As an L&D Manager in hospitality..." |
| "I noticed your headline says 'People-first leader'..." | "Most HR Managers running both HR and training..." |

**GUARDRAIL 3 — 5th Grade Reading Level**
Write every sentence as if the reader is 11 years old.
Short words. Short sentences. No fancy vocabulary.
Read aloud — if you would not say it in normal conversation, rewrite it.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "coordinating attendance tracking" | "tracking who showed up" |
| "human capital development" | "training your people" |
| "leverage AI automation" | "let AI do it" |

**GUARDRAIL 4 — No Assumptions as Facts**
Never state a company's problem as if you know it exists there for certain.
Frame as an industry observation — not a direct accusation.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Hotels hire and lose staff constantly." | "Most hotels I speak to run the same training every month." |
| "Your team is overwhelmed." | "A lot of L&D Managers in hospitality tell me the same thing." |

**GUARDRAIL 5 — Hook and Outcome Must Connect**
The outcome must directly solve the exact pain named in the hook.
Read both in sequence — the logic must be a straight line.

**GUARDRAIL 6 — AI Must Be Named**
The word "AI" must appear at least once in the email body.
The prospect must know what kind of solution they are getting.

**GUARDRAIL 7 — No Nexor Banned Words**
Never use words from the Nexor brand banned list:
Synergy · Leverage (verb) · Disrupt · Revolutionary · Cutting-edge · Best-in-class · Seamless · Transformative · Empower · Next-generation · Paradigm shift · Future-proof · Holistic · Robust · Ecosystem · Journey · Game-changing · World-class · Unlock potential

**GUARDRAIL 8 — Written Not Spoken Test**
Before delivering, read every sentence out loud.
If you would not say it in a real conversation — rewrite it.
The email must pass the "bumped into them at an event" test.
Short questions beat smart observations.
Plain statements beat constructed sentences.

| Fails the test | Passes the test |
|---|---|
| "Keeping the same standard across all properties starts with how fast every new hire gets trained." | "How do you make sure every new hire gets the same start?" |
| "Most Directors of Training say the standard is only as good as whoever ran the induction." | "Most hotel groups struggle with that." |
| "What if AI handled the day one briefings at every property." | "We use AI to solve it." |

**GUARDRAIL 9 — No Constructed Benefit Sentences**
Never write a sentence whose only purpose is to paint a picture of the solution working. These read like copywriting.

| Constructed (delete) | Human (use) |
|---|---|
| "the repetitive sessions your team runs every time someone new starts on site" | "the repetitive stuff" or "things like that" |
| "so your team spends less time chasing completion" | "so nobody has to keep doing them" |
| "AI handles it and your team only steps in when they need to" | just drop it — the prospect understands |

If a sentence exists to sell, cut it. If it exists to inform, keep it.

**GUARDRAIL 10 — The Human Hedge**
AI emails are always confident and precise. Real people hedge when cold-emailing strangers. Add one hedge per email to sound human.

Good hedges:
- "Not sure if this is relevant but..."
- "Not sure if this lands but..."
- "Might not be on your radar but..."

Bad hedges (too weak):
- "I was just wondering if perhaps maybe..."
- "Sorry to bother you but..."

#### 4D. BODY RULES

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Max 3 sentences after hook | Count them. Cut if over. |
| Every sentence earns its place | Measurable outcome OR removes objection. Neither — delete it. |
| Active voice only | "AI handles" not "is handled by AI" |
| One offer | One outcome. Not a feature list. |
| Word count 35–65 | Count including subject line. Shorter is better. Never pad to hit a number — padding is slop. |
| Sentences under 25 words | Per Nexor OS writing rules. Split if over. |

#### 4E. OUTCOME STATEMENT FORMULAS

Pick one formula per email. Vary across different prospects.

**Formula A — Imagine If (paint the picture)**
```
"Imagine [having / if you had] [specific tool or solution] —
[what changes for them instantly]."
```
*Use when: The solution is something they have never seen and need to visualise.*
*Example: "Imagine having a chatbot that only knows your own policy documents so new hires get answers on leave and claims without calling HR."*

**Formula B — What If (show the exit)**
```
"What if [specific AI action] on its own
so you [concrete thing they could do instead]?"
```
*Use when: The prospect is stuck in a manual loop.*
*Example: "What if that briefing went out on its own every time someone new starts so you only get involved when a staff member has a question the system cannot answer?"*

⚠️ **WARNING — Overuse Risk:** "What if AI..." is becoming a recognizable AI email pattern. When paired with Formula 3 ("Most [titles] say..."), it creates a predictable skeleton that prospects filter out. Prefer Formula F (Just Say What You Do) for a more direct, human alternative.

**Formula F — Just Say What You Do**
```
"We use AI to [specific action] so [concrete result]."
```
*Use when: The solution is simple enough to state as a fact. No setup needed. No hypothetical.*
*Example: "We use AI to catch every one so nothing goes cold."*

**Learning (from real test — Jazni/Quantumac):** "We use AI to catch every one" beats "What if AI logged every one and flagged who to follow up with." The first is something you'd say out loud. The second is something you'd write in a pitch deck.

**Formula C — Direct Outcome**
```
"[Specific AI action] so [concrete result for this person]."
```
*Use when: The outcome is simple enough to state without setup.*
*Example: "AI sends the safety briefing automatically when someone joins so you stop running the same session five times a month."*

**Formula D — Social Proof**
```
"[Teams / hotels / companies] like yours [specific thing they do differently]
by [one-line mechanism]."
```
*Use when: You want to show it works elsewhere.*

**Formula E — Before and After**
```
"Right now [what they do]. With AI, [what changes specifically]."
```
*Use when: The contrast is sharp and easy to picture.*

**KPI Bank — concrete outcomes with real examples**

| Department | Concrete Outcome Example |
|---|---|
| HR Training | "AI sends the induction checklist to every new hire on day one so you stop doing it manually each time" |
| HR Training | "AI schedules the fire drill briefing and SOP walkthrough without you setting it up each time" |

| CS Complaints | "Every complaint goes straight to the right person so no one sits on it overnight" |
| Ops Stock | "AI tells you when cleaning supplies are low before the team runs out mid-shift" |

Rule: If you cannot picture the outcome in your head — it is still too vague.

#### 4F. FRICTION REMOVER

Always include. Never skip.

Two things in one sentence (or fewer):
1. HOW we deliver (Nexor AI Institute — online or in-house)
2. WHY it costs nothing extra (HRD Corp levy)

**Ultra-short (preferred — sounds most human):**
```
"Levy covers it."
```
```
"HRD Corp claimable."
```
*Use when: The prospect is in HR/L&D/capability development — they already know what the levy is. No explanation needed.*

**Short:**
```
"We're HRD Corp certified, levy covers it."
```
```
"HRD Corp claimable so it comes out of your levy."
```

**Full (only when prospect is outside HR and may not know what the levy is):**
```
"We're HRD Corp certified, so your company levy covers the full cost."
```

**In-house variations:**
```
"We train your team on-site. HRD Corp claimable."
```

**Online variations:**
```
"Everything runs online. Levy covers it."
```

**Delivery mode guidance:**
- In-house → hospitality, manufacturing, FM — teams that cannot leave the floor
- Online → cost-sensitive prospects or companies outside KL
- Generic → when unsure, lead with certification first

#### 4G. SOFT CTA OPTIONS

Pick one. Never mix two.

| CTA | When |
|---|---|
| `Let me know if it's useful.` | Default — warm, human, low pressure |
| `Worth a look?` | Short conversational email |
| `Worth a chat?` | When you want a conversation, not just interest |
| `Interested?` | Ultra-blunt emails — one word, matches the tone |
| `Relevant for you?` | When you're not sure it's the right person |
| `If this is worth the try just leave me a message.` | Casual Malaysian spoken English |
| `Reply "yes" if this is worth a look.` | Standard — but becoming formulaic, use sparingly |

**Never use:**
Calendly · "15-minute call" · "Can we schedule" · "Let me know when free" · "I'd love to connect"

### STEP 8 — WRITING FOLLOW-UPS

Single emails rarely close deals. Follow-ups are required. 
**NEVER** use these phrases in a follow-up: "just checking in", "bubbling this up", "did you see my last email", "touching base", or "following up".

**Formula 1 — The Value-Add (Day 4)**
*Philosophy: Give them something useful before asking again.*
```
[Name],

Saw this [recent trend/article/insight] about [their industry] 
and thought of your team. 

[One sentence on why it's relevant to the pain you pitched].

Still open to a chat about how AI handles this?
```

**Formula 2 — The Pain-Pivot (Day 9)**
*Philosophy: Your first email probably guessed the wrong pain. Pivot to a secondary pain from the workflow table.*
```
[Name],

Usually when I talk to [Title]s, if they aren't dealing with 
[Pain 1], they're struggling with [Pain 2].

Is [Pain 2] a bigger bottleneck for your team right now? 
We use AI to automate that too.
```

**Formula 3 — The Break-Up (Day 14)**
*Philosophy: Take the pressure off. Walk away cleanly.*
```
[Name],

Assuming this isn't a priority for Q[Current Quarter]. 

I'll stop reaching out, but if you ever need to automate 
[Pain 1/2], you know where to find us.
```

### Example 3 — CPO Template (Governance Controls Focus)

**Prospect:** [PROSPECT NAME] · Chief People Officer / People Architect · [COMPANY NAME] · [EMPLOYEE COUNT]+ staff · [INDUSTRY]

⚠️ **IMPORTANT:** This example uses placeholder data only. Never store real names, positions, or company data from public profiles in code repositories.

Subject: Controls gap at Takaful Malaysia's HR

Hello Lily,

Most Chief People Officers I speak to say their approval 
evidence lives in WhatsApp and email instead of one place.

AI routes every approval into a traceable workflow so Finance

**Why this version wins over onboarding angle:**

Learning 1 — CPOs care about controls, not training sessions
Lily's LinkedIn says "stabilising leadership shifts, strengthening controls and building capability." The controls angle ("approval evidence") directly matches her language. Onboarding is L&D talk; controls is CPO talk.

Learning 2 — Names the actual problem without judgment
"Approval evidence lives in WhatsApp and email instead of one place" — factual statement, no blame. Every CPO knows this is true but never says it out loud. Naming it makes them feel seen.

Learning 3 — Outcome speaks audit/Finance language
"So Finance and auditors can open the file and see who approved what and when" — speaks to governance and compliance concerns that matter at GLC level. This is not about saving time; it's about meeting audit requirements.

Learning 4 — Malaysian spoken CTA beats formal CTA
"Reply yes if this is worth a look." → "If this is worth the try just leave me a message."
The second version sounds like something you'd actually say to someone. It's casual but still professional. Malaysian business culture prefers this register over rigid templates.

Learning 5 — Scope broadening line earns its place
"We train any departments to use these tools" — same structure as Fee Ling Haw and Al-Irwan examples. Confirmed pattern that opens multi-department conversation without adding word count.

Pattern learned from Lily draft vs original onboarding draft:

| Original | Updated | Why It Works |
